# Foundations of Interaction Literacy

[Start here](README.md) · Status: v0.1 · Formative / educational

Interaction Literacy asks what a person must understand and practice to remain an active participant in cognition when work is distributed between people and machines. Here it is an educational framework developed for Trivian Institute, not a validated competency standard. Its normative commitments are agency, discernment, consent, and non-domination. Learners may question how those commitments should be interpreted.

An interaction includes a human purpose, a system's capabilities and limits, an interface, the information available, institutional rules, and people affected by the outcome. Prompt wording matters, but cannot by itself determine any of those conditions. A polished answer is only one part of an interaction.

## Three questions that organize the first release

1. What is shaping this exchange, and what would count as success?
2. What can happen, who may authorize it, and who remains answerable?
3. What supports this claim, and what would change our confidence?

These questions are useful when a system produces text, collaborates on a project, retains context, or performs delegated actions. Not every AI system has memory or action access. Adapt examples to the actual system, rather than attributing capabilities to all AI.

## How to read claims in this curriculum

| Kind | What it means here | Appropriate treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empirical evidence | An observation or study with identifiable methods | Ask about design, population, limitations, and replication |
| Established knowledge | A well-supported finding within a stated scope | Avoid treating it as universal or permanent |
| Interpretation | A reasoned account of what facts mean | Compare plausible alternatives |
| Trivian framework | An organizing proposal from this educational program | Evaluate usefulness and blind spots |
| Philosophical proposition | A claim about value, meaning, or responsibility | Examine premises and consequences |
| Hypothesis | A proposition requiring testing | Specify a possible disconfirming result |
| Practice | An activity offered for learning | Observe experience without claiming treatment or guaranteed benefit |
| Open question | Something these materials do not settle | Preserve uncertainty and identify needed inquiry |

The distinctions “capability is not authority” and “fluency is not accuracy” separate concepts. They are not measurements of a system's overall quality. A fluent answer may be accurate; an authorized action may still be unwise. Each needs its own evaluation.

## Relationship without careless attribution

A person may experience an exchange as significant. That experience can be discussed seriously without deciding that a system has human feelings, reciprocal commitments, or consciousness. Conversely, uncertainty about a system's inner experience does not erase the human experience or the institutional responsibilities surrounding it.

Non-domination is a normative orientation: people affected by a decision should have meaningful ways to question and contest it. The curriculum asks who has that opportunity and who lacks it. It does not assume that an individual user's preference settles every obligation to others.

## Learning and assessment

Learners demonstrate their reasoning through decisions, evidence checks, boundaries, and revisions. They are not assessed on agreement with Trivian language, willingness to engage with AI, emotional disclosure, or physical participation. All initial exercises can run without an AI account.
