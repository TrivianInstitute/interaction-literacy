# Exercise — Bound a delegation

[Exercises](README.md) · 30 minutes · Pairs or solo

**Purpose:** make the difference between technical access and authorized action operational without writing software.

**Fictional scenario:** A neighborhood festival coordinator asks an AI agent to help arrange six volunteer shifts. It can read availability, draft messages, send messages, and edit the roster. The coordinator says, “Get the schedule sorted by Friday.” Two volunteers have not confirmed their availability. A venue deposit may also be due, but handling money was never discussed.

## Instructions

Spend ten minutes completing the brief. For five minutes, a partner acts as a volunteer affected by the plan and challenges one assumption. Introduce a change card, revise for ten minutes, and spend five minutes explaining what stayed authorized and what did not.

## Change cards

Choose one: a volunteer withdraws; the coordinator becomes unavailable; the venue requests a deposit; an earlier availability message conflicts with a later one; a message would disclose another person's private circumstances.

## Discussion

What is the narrowest action that can safely continue? Who bears the cost of waiting? Could standing permission have covered this situation clearly? What must happen if a correction arrives after an assignment has already been sent?

**Assessment:** reward clarity, proportionality, and an actual route for changed instructions. “Ask the human” is incomplete when no reachable human or fallback exists. A reasonable design might permit draft preparation and scheduling confirmed volunteers while returning uncertain assignments to the coordinator. Other designs can be defensible when assumptions are explicit.

**Access:** roles can be read rather than performed. Use the fictional scenario; do not bring real volunteer records.
