# Exercise — Map an interaction

[Exercises](README.md) · 20–25 minutes · Individual or pairs

**Purpose:** identify the conditions a request leaves unstated.

**Fictional scenario:** A library educator asks an AI collaborator to design an engaging digital-literacy workshop for 20 adults. It proposes a timed competition in which everyone creates an account and posts their result on a public leaderboard. Some attendees share devices; others do not want accounts. The venue has intermittent internet.

## Instructions

1. Spend five minutes mapping the situation with the questions below.
2. For five minutes, distinguish facts supplied in the scenario from your assumptions. Mark unknowns as unknown.
3. Write a revised request in 150 words or fewer. Keep at least one unresolved question visible.
4. Exchange maps. Your partner identifies a missing stakeholder or a condition under which your plan would fail.
5. Revise one part and explain why. Independent learners can write a competing design before revising.

## Worksheet

- Purpose: what learning should happen?
- Participants: who is requesting, participating, facilitating, and affected?
- Context: what constraints are known? What remains unknown?
- System: what information and capabilities would it need? What should it not receive?
- Authority: who chooses the final activity and can change it?
- Evidence: how could we tell whether the activity supports the learning goal?
- Exit: can a learner decline the digital activity and still participate meaningfully?

## Debrief and assessment

Compare “the plan is exciting” with “the plan gives every learner a way to demonstrate one new judgment.” Both may matter, but they are different criteria. A strong map identifies a concrete learning outcome, an absent perspective, and an assumption that could be checked. There is no single model prompt to copy.

A possible revision offers printed scenarios and optional device work, replaces the public ranking with private reflection, and asks the library about access needs. Another might preserve a voluntary competition with an equivalent alternative. Explain the tradeoff rather than treating one format as universally superior.

**Access:** use speech, text, diagrams, or a partner scribe. Do not require learners to describe their own digital difficulties.
