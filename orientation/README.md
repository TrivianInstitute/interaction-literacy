# Understanding AI in Everyday Life

[Interaction Literacy home](../README.md) · Beginner pathway · No AI account needed

**Status:** v0.1 draft · Original educational materials · Not piloted  
**Audience:** adults and older secondary learners, with adaptation by a facilitator where appropriate  
**Time:** four lessons totaling about 110 minutes, plus a 20-minute final task; breaks and access time are additional  
**Materials:** these pages and a way to make notes; paper, a private document, dictation, drawing, or discussion all work

AI can help us discover, compose, translate, plan, and act. Participating well begins with knowing what is happening in a particular exchange. A ranked list, a generated explanation, and an action taken through an account call for different questions, even when the same service provides all three.

This pathway offers an accessible introduction to the mechanisms and choices behind those experiences. It prepares learners to enter the existing Interaction Literacy lessons on [framing](../curriculum/01-from-prompting-to-interaction.md), [delegation](../curriculum/02-capability-authority-delegation.md), and [verification](../curriculum/03-verification.md). It requires no coding, specialist vocabulary, purchased service, or personal account history.

## What you will be able to do

After working through the activities, you should be able to:

1. Identify when a described service is ranking, predicting, generating, or taking action, and recognize combinations.
2. Explain the difference between learning during training, generating an answer, receiving current context, finding a source, and storing information for later.
3. Ask what data and design choices shape an output, whose priorities a success measure serves, and what information should remain private.
4. Evaluate a proposed use of AI with attention to affected people, access, work, resources, responsibility, and a way to challenge an outcome.

These are proposed learning goals. Completion shows the reasoning demonstrated in these activities; it does not establish lasting competence or the effectiveness of the pathway.

## Choose a route

| Lesson | Guiding question | Time | What you make |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [01. Recognizing AI](01-recognizing-ai.md) | What is the system doing here? | 25 min | A function map and one question that resolves an uncertainty |
| [02. From training to an answer](02-from-training-to-answer.md) | Where could this information have come from? | 30 min | An information map and a corrected answer with its basis explained |
| [03. Data, design, and incentives](03-data-design-incentives.md) | What is being optimized, and who is missing? | 25 min | A revised recommendation design and a minimal-data decision |
| [04. AI in a shared world](04-ai-in-a-shared-world.md) | What would make this a responsible shared arrangement? | 30 min | A bounded adoption or non-adoption proposal |
| [Final transfer task](workbook.md#final-transfer-task--20-minutes) | Can I investigate an unfamiliar service? | 20 min | An independent response, then a reasoned revision |

For self-study, work in order. Read each situation, make an initial response, and then consult the feedback section. You can keep the [workbook](workbook.md) beside you or respond directly in your own format. Use the [glossary](glossary.md) when a term is unfamiliar. The [sources and alignment note](sources.md) is available for deeper checking; opening external pages is optional for completing the activities.

For a group, a facilitator can read the situations aloud and invite private responses before discussion. The time estimates are planning aids, not speed requirements. Each lesson includes a complete task and guidance for comparing answers. Two shorter sittings may work better than one longer meeting.

## Participation and access

All people, organizations, records, products, and results in the worked cases are fictional. No learner needs to reveal their own messages, habits, diagnosis, relationships, workplace information, or account settings. Do not upload personal material to demonstrate a point.

Offer written, spoken, drawn, dictated, or observational participation. Reading support, translation support, assistive technology, extra time, and a scribe remain available during independent tasks. Independence means the learner supplies the judgment; it does not mean removing access supports. Someone may pass on a discussion, take a break, or choose the paper route without penalty. A facilitator should check translations of key distinctions rather than assume automated translation preserves them.

The pathway takes human experience of relationship with machine intelligence seriously. It examines available mechanisms and observable behavior without using conversational warmth to settle questions about inner experience. Learners can disagree about a proposed use of AI and still demonstrate careful reasoning. See the [existing foundations](../FOUNDATIONS.md) for the Trivian educational stance.

## Feedback and next steps

Keep one artifact from each lesson and complete the final unfamiliar case. Notice whether your explanation names a mechanism, a relevant uncertainty, an affected person, and a condition that would change your decision. Use the feedback to revise a specific omission. There is no total score or pass certificate.

Then continue into the three Interaction Literacy modules, or follow the [shared AI-literacy and embodied-digital-life route](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/educator-commons/blob/main/pathways/ai-literacy-and-embodied-digital-life.md). Apply the ideas through the [Relational AI Casebook](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/relational-ai-casebook), [Interactive Dialogue](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/interactive-dialogue), and [Embodied Digital Life](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/embodiment-commons/tree/main/courses/embodied-digital-life). Younger learners have a dedicated [Youth Interaction Literacy pathway](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/youth-interaction-literacy).
