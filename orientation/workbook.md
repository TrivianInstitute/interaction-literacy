# Learner workbook and final transfer task

[Pathway](README.md) · Reusable prompts · No account or personal disclosure required

Use paper, a private document, speech, drawing, or dictation. You can answer with short phrases and explain them to a partner. There is no required total word count. A scribe, reading support, translation support, assistive technology, extra time, and breaks can be used throughout. Keep your work private unless you choose an appropriate sharing arrangement.

These prompts support reasoning; they are not a validated assessment or a certificate of AI literacy. The final task concerns an unfamiliar fictional setting. It lets you notice which questions you can generate without the earlier worksheets, while retaining access supports.

## Final transfer task — 20 minutes

### 1. Respond to an unfamiliar case — 7 minutes

**Fictional case: the festival guide.** A local arts festival proposes a service that helps visitors choose a route. Its product sheet says it uses a trained model to rank exhibits by predicted time spent at the festival, and a language model to generate descriptions. It retrieves information from last season's catalog and this season's access list. Visitors can save preferences for later. It can send a route invitation to a contact, but only after the visitor approves the recipient and message. No purchase function is included.

The prototype says, “This route is ideal for you. I remember that you prefer quiet spaces, and the garden installation is open until 18:00.” The product sheet does not say whether the prototype received the quiet-space preference from the current conversation or a saved record. The festival has a printed catalog, but the prototype's route map has no text alternative. Staff have not yet planned who will correct descriptions or answer challenges. No service-specific resource estimate is supplied.

**Your task:** advise the festival on what should happen before offering this service to visitors. Explain what you can conclude, what you would investigate, and your proposed next step. Begin in your own structure without consulting the earlier workbook tables or the feedback below. Keep the case text, glossary, and any usual access supports available. This is an independent judgment task, not a memory test.

You may recommend a limited version, revisions before evaluation, a different approach, or postponement. Your recommendation should make its reasons visible.

### 2. Consider new information — 5 minutes

Read after making an initial response. The following update is also fictional:

- The current access list says the garden installation closes at 16:00; 18:00 appeared in last season's catalog.
- The visitor's current conversation includes “I prefer quiet spaces.” Saving preferences is disabled for this visitor.
- A staff member explains that the quiet route includes a stairway; an alternative accessible route is available in the current list but absent from the prototype's map.
- The visitor withdraws permission to send an invitation before any message is sent.

Revise your advice. Identify which conclusion changed, which became clearer, and which remains uncertain. Explain why an approved message must now remain unsent. Do not claim that the whole system is fixed just because you have identified the problems in this example.

### 3. Compare with the feedback guide — 6 minutes

Look for the following reasoning in your response. The wording and final recommendation can differ.

| Dimension | Evidence of reasoning in this task | If it is missing, ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanism | Distinguishes ranking, generated descriptions, retrieval, and sending an invitation. | Which functions are actually described, and which are assumed? |
| Information | Treats the old catalog and current access list as different sources; does not infer saved memory from wording. | What explains the available preference, and what establishes the current opening time? |
| Purpose | Asks whether predicted time at the festival serves the visitor's priorities. | Could someone have a valuable short visit? |
| Access and representation | Identifies the map's missing text route and the stairway; asks how visitors can reach equivalent information. | Who encounters a barrier, and who can help assess a revision? |
| Authority and repair | Stops the withdrawn invitation, assigns a correction owner, and checks revised output against current records. | Who can act, correct, or challenge the arrangement? |
| Wider effects | Asks about staff work and scoped resource evidence without inventing a footprint. | What would need to be known to compare this with alternatives? |
| Revision | Connects a changed recommendation to new facts and leaves unresolved questions visible. | What changed your mind, and what still needs investigation? |

**One defensible response:** “Keep the invitation unsent. Correct the garden hours against the current access list and provide the alternative route in readable text. The quiet-space preference is present in this conversation, so this example does not demonstrate saved memory. Before visitor use, assign record maintenance and correction work, test routes with relevant access needs, and examine whether the ranking serves visitors' purposes. Request scoped information about logs and resources, and compare a limited assistant with improving the existing directory.”

That response is an example, not the only acceptable decision. A learner who favors postponement could reason equally well. A learner who proposes proceeding immediately needs to address the documented wrong hours, access barrier, and missing correction arrangement.

Do not sum the dimensions into an accuracy or competence percentage. A single task cannot establish durable learning or a causal benefit from the course. It can show a particular reasoning move, an omission to revisit, or a question the material should explain better.

### 4. Keep one revision — 2 minutes

Record one change to your response and the reason for it. Then finish:

“In a future unfamiliar interaction, the first question I want to remember is ___, because ___.”

## Optional later revisit

On another occasion, choose a different fictional service and explain what you would investigate before using it. Keep access supports available and note which questions arise without prompting. This is a reflection opportunity, not an obligation or an effectiveness study. If a facilitator wants to report teaching feedback, use [Learning in Practice](https://github.com/TrivianInstitute/learning-in-practice) and its privacy boundaries; do not publish this workbook with learner names, private responses, or identifying details.
