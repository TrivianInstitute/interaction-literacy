These are primary institutional sources, not validation of this curriculum. Links checked during drafting on 2026-10-01. Read the original document and relevant section before extending a claim.

- [NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile (2024)](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1). Its discussion of confabulation supports the narrow claim that confidently stated false content is a recognized generative-AI risk. Its human–AI configuration category provides questions about overreliance and interaction. A risk taxonomy does not measure the prevalence of a risk in a particular classroom.
- [NIST, AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework). Institutional context for identifying and managing risks. Read as a governance framework, not a lesson plan or proof of educational effectiveness.
- [UNESCO, Guidance for generative AI in education and research (2023)](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research). A policy-oriented starting point for human agency, inclusion, and educational appropriateness. Recommendations are not experimental findings.

## Reading protocol

Record the claim you are investigating, the source date, the passage or section that bears on it, its scope, and a limitation. Seek evidence that might challenge your interpretation. For changing product behavior, consult current provider documentation and test only within authorized, low-stakes conditions. Do not infer persistent memory or action access from conversational style.

## Unresolved research agenda

The Commons has not established that its activities improve long-term judgment, reduce dependency, or produce safer decisions. Useful next studies would compare alternative pedagogies, assess transfer without prompting, include disabled and multilingual learners, and measure workload alongside benefits.
