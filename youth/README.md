# Youth Interaction Literacy: first activities

Status: v0.1 draft · Formative educational resources · Not piloted

This pathway helps young people notice how AI affects their thinking, relationships, privacy, and choices. It extends [Interaction Literacy](../README.md) with age-responsive activities. Ages are starting points, not tests of ability; facilitators may adjust reading level, examples, duration, and response mode.

| Audience | Start here | Inquiry | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Families and educators, including ages 6–9 | [Companion guide](companion-guide.md) | How do we explore minds, machines, and choices together? | 15–30 min |
| Ages 10–12 | [The answer with missing pieces](ages-10-12-answer-with-missing-pieces.md) | What must I check before repeating an answer? | 25–35 min |
| Ages 13–15 | [The recommendation that knows me](ages-13-15-recommendation-and-agency.md) | Who benefits from this recommendation, and what can I choose? | 35–45 min |
| Ages 16–18 | [The council and the AI brief](ages-16-18-council-and-ai-brief.md) | What authority may we delegate, and how can we contest a decision? | 45–60 min |

The companion guide may accompany the future book project *Me, My Mind & the Machine*, but requires no book and makes no claims about its unpublished story. No manuscript, illustrations, excerpts, or proprietary book assets are included here.

## Shared conditions

- All scenarios are fictional and work on paper. No learner needs a live AI account or paid access.
- Do not ask young people to enter names, private experiences, photos, schoolwork, or other identifying information into an AI service. Follow local policies, platform age rules, and parental or school permissions if a facilitator chooses any live demonstration.
- Permit speaking, writing, drawing, or observing. A learner may pass, leave a reflection private, or choose an alternative to a personal question.
- Invite disagreement and multiple defensible answers. Assess the reasoning and care shown, not agreement with a preferred position or willingness to interact with AI.
- Ask youth for feedback on clarity and relevance without collecting or publishing identifiable stories.

## What is being tested

These activities have not been reviewed with youth, caregivers, teachers, or accessibility specialists. Try them with appropriate oversight, record non-identifying feedback, and revise before claiming effectiveness or curricular validation. For context, see [UNESCO's AI Competency Framework for Students](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students) and [UNICEF's Guidance on AI and Children](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children). Those frameworks inform questions about progression and child rights; they do not endorse this pathway.
